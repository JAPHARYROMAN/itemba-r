## CRITICAL severity

### [CRITICAL] Reversed journal entries are double-counted as negatives in every financial report/statement

- **Domain:** Accounting engine & GL  |  **Type:** correctness  |  **File:** `backend/src/modules/financial-reports/financial-reports.service.ts:89`
- **Evidence:** All aggregation queries filter `journalEntry: { status: 'POSTED' }` (getCompanySummary L27, getTrialBalance L89, getScopeRollup L149, getProfitAndLoss L222, getBalanceSheet L289, getCashFlow L683, getGroupTrialBalance L863, getIntercompanyJournalEntryIds L1348), as does financial-statements.service.ts generate() (L48). But the system-wide reversal convention (journal-entries.service.ts reverse() L497-503; and identically in credit-notes L697-699, customer-payments L611-613, refunds L561-563, sales-orders L2295-2297, payroll-postings) flips the ORIGINAL entry to status='REVERSED' AND posts a separate reversal entry with flipped debit/credit at status='POSTED' (postLines defaults status to POSTED). Reports therefore EXCLUDE the REVERSED original but INCLUDE the flipped reversal. No report uses reversedAt/reversalOfId to compensate (confirmed: no matches in financial-reports.service.ts).
- **Failure scenario:** Company posts a sale JE (DR AR 118,000 / CR Revenue 100,000 / CR VAT 18,000), status POSTED. It is later reversed (JE reverse, or void credit note, or reversed customer payment). Original -> REVERSED; reversal JE (DR Revenue 100,000 / DR VAT 18,000 / CR AR 118,000) -> POSTED. Trial Balance, P&L and Balance Sheet (status='POSTED') now show ONLY the reversal: Revenue = -100,000, AR = -118,000, VAT payable = -18,000. The correct net is zero. Every reversed transaction pushes the statements negative by its full amount, so financial statements are materially wrong whenever any reversal exists.
- **Suggested fix:** Reporting must be consistent with the reversal model. Either (a) include REVERSED originals in report queries so original+reversal net to zero: change the filter to `status: { in: ['POSTED','REVERSED'] }` everywhere in financial-reports.service.ts and financial-statements.service.ts; or (b) stop flipping the original to REVERSED and instead leave both original and reversal POSTED (the model audit-adjustments already uses). Pick one convention and apply it uniformly across posters and reports.
- **Verifier:** Verified every link in the chain against the code. (1) Reversal convention: journal-entries.service.ts reverse() atomically flips the original to status='REVERSED' (L498-503) then creates a reversal entry with status:'POSTED' (L529) whose lines swap debit/credit (debit: Number(line.credit), credit: Number(line.debit), L542-543). The sibling modules do the same: credit-notes.service.ts reverseCreditNoteJournal claims original -> REVERSED (L699) then postLines (L712); customer-payments.service.ts reversePaymentJournal claims REVERSED (L613) then postLines (L626). (2) postLines always sets status:'POSTED' (posting-engine.service.ts L91). (3) schema.prisma L1835-1840 confirms JournalEntryStatus has REVERSED as a value DISTINCT from POSTED, so a 'POSTED' filter truly excludes it. (4) Every report/statement query filters journalEntry status to exactly 'POSTED': financial-reports.service.ts L27, L89 (trial balance), L149 (scope rollup), L222 (P&L), L289 (balance sheet), L683 (cash flow), L863 (group TB), L1348 (intercompany); financial-statements.service.ts generate() L48. (5) Confirmed by grep that no report references REVERSED/reversalOfId/reversedAt to compensate (zero matches in the financial-reports module). Net effect: the REVERSED original contributes 0 to reports while the POSTED mirror contributes its full negative. Concrete trace: original sale (DR AR 118,000/CR Revenue 100,000/CR VAT 18,000) -> REVERSED (excluded); reversal (DR Revenue 100,000/DR VAT 18,000/CR AR 118,000) -> POSTED (included). P&L income (sum credit-debit over INCOME lines, L243/246) = 0 (excluded original) + (0-100,000) = -100,000, when the economically correct post-reversal value is 0. Balance sheet AR = -118,000, VAT payable = -18,000, all wrong by the full amount. This is a genuine double-remove: the design should EITHER keep the original POSTED so it nets against the mirror, OR include REVERSED entries in reports; it does neither. No test in the financial-reports spec covers reversal (grep: no matches), so nothing enforces correct behavior. Not covered by any KNOWN INTENDED item. The auditor's reading and arithmetic are correct.

### [CRITICAL] 'Pending for me' returns all pending requests globally — no user filter and no company scope (cross-company leak)

### [CRITICAL] Payroll accrual JE is unbalanced when any employee has a non-statutory deduction or salary-advance recovery — approval aborts

- **Domain:** HR & payroll  |  **Type:** broken  |  **File:** `backend/src/modules/hr/payroll-postings/payroll-postings.service.ts:198-226`
- **Evidence:** postRun() debits SALARIES_EXPENSE = totals.grossPay (full gross of every entry) and credits only the statutory payables plus SALARIES_PAYABLE = totals.netPay. But netPay persisted on each PayrollEntry (payroll-runs.service.ts:429-434) is grossPay - totalEmployeeStatutory - cappedNonStatutory - totalAdvanceRecovery. The credit side therefore equals (employeeStatutory + net) = gross - cappedNonStatutory - totalAdvanceRecovery, i.e. total credits fall short of total debits (gross) by exactly (nonStatutory + advanceRecovery). There is NO credit line for non-statutory manual deductions or advance recovery (ACCOUNT_CODES has no 'other deductions payable' / employee-receivable credit). The balance guard at line 222 (abs diff > 0.01) then throws BadRequestException and the whole approve/postRun transaction rolls back.
- **Failure scenario:** Employee has base salary 1,000,000 with an ACTIVE non-statutory 'housing loan' deduction of 50,000 (or an outstanding salary advance whose installment recovers 50,000 this period). calculate() stores netPay = gross - statutory - 50,000. On approve()/approveFinance(), postRun() builds Dr Salaries Expense 1,000,000 vs credits that total 950,000; line 222 throws 'Journal entry would not balance: Dr ... vs Cr ...'. The payroll run can never move past CALCULATED/SUBMITTED — payroll is fully blocked for any company that uses advances or any non-statutory deduction.
- **Suggested fix:** Add credit lines for the missing amounts: credit Employee Receivable (1110) for the total advance recovery (relieving the advance asset) and credit an 'Other Payroll Deductions Payable' account for capped non-statutory deductions. Sum these into the accrual so total credits = gross. Aggregate totalAdvanceRecovery and totalNonStatutory from PayrollEntryDeduction rows (salaryAdvanceId != null vs the rest) in the same loop that reads entries.
- **Verifier:** The finding's arithmetic is airtight and defensible against the actual code.

1. Persisted netPay is short-form. In payroll-runs.service.ts, totalDeductionsEmployee = totalEmployeeStatutory + cappedNonStatutory + totalAdvanceRecovery (lines 429-433), and netPay = grossPay - totalDeductionsEmployee (line 434). The payrollEntry is created with THIS netPay (line 448), not the calculator's breakdown.netPay (which is gross - statutory only). So each entry's netPay = gross - statutory - cappedNonStatutory - totalAdvanceRecovery.

2. The accrual JE (postRun, payroll-postings.service.ts) debits SALARIES_EXPENSE = totals.grossPay (line 199, sum of e.grossPay) and, on the employee side, credits only the statutory payables (PAYE/NSSF-ee/PSSSF-ee/NHIF-ee/HESLB) whose sum equals totals... which equals the calculator's totalEmployeeStatutory (calculator lines 212-214 sum employeeContribution over the exact same lines), plus SALARIES_PAYABLE = totals.netPay (line 218). Employer expense debits (200-204) are exactly matched by the employer portion baked into the payable credits (208,211,214,215,216), so they net to zero and do not mask the shortfall.

3. Employee-side balance therefore is: Dr gross vs Cr (statutory + net) = statutory + (gross - statutory - cappedNonStatutory - totalAdvanceRecovery) = gross - cappedNonStatutory - totalAdvanceRecovery. Credits fall short of debits by exactly cappedNonStatutory + totalAdvanceRecovery.

4. There is no credit line for non-statutory deductions or advance recovery: ACCOUNT_CODES (lines 31-48) has no 'other deductions payable' code, and grep confirms postRun never references the 1110 Employee Receivable or any 22xx other-payable. No other posting (postPayment, postAdvancePayment, postLabourReclass) credits the shortfall either.

5. The balance guard at line 222 throws BadRequestException when |Dr - Cr| > 0.01. postRun is called inside the approve/approveHr/approveFinance transactions (lines 681, 724, 772), so the throw rolls back the whole approval and the run is stuck in SUBMITTED.

Failure scenario is concrete and common: an employee with base salary 1,000,000 and one ACTIVE non-statutory EmployeeDeduction (e.g. housing loan) of 50,000, or an outstanding SalaryAdvance recovering 50,000 this period, yields Dr 1,000,000 vs Cr 950,000 -> guard throws 'Journal entry would not balance'. Any company using advances or any non-statutory deduction cannot approve payroll.

Not a KNOWN INTENDED item. Not caught by tests: there is no spec for payroll-postings.service (postRun), and the dual-signoff spec mocks postRun entirely (line 86), so the real balance path is never exercised with a non-statutory deduction/advance. The auditor did not misread; behavior is not handled elsewhere. Confirmed critical.

### [CRITICAL] receivables.recordPayment reduces AR subledger + customer balance but posts NO journal entry (GL AR control never credited)

- **Domain:** Receivables & customer money  |  **Type:** missing-logic  |  **File:** `backend/src/modules/receivables/receivables.service.ts:257-333`
- **Evidence:** recordPayment locks the receivable, decrements outstandingAmount/paidAmount, sets status PAID/PARTIALLY_PAID, calls syncSalesOrderPaymentFromReceivable and syncCustomerBalance — but never calls postingEngine.postLines and the DTO (record-receivable-payment.dto.ts) has no cashAccountId. No cash is debited and AR is not credited in the GL. The direct sibling payables.service.ts:310-344 explicitly posts DR AP / CR Cash on payment ('ITMB-045: post the balanced settlement entry so AP control reduces'), and customer-payments.service.ts:278-292 posts DR Cash / CR AR. Receivables is the unfinished mirror. Because no JE is posted, this path also bypasses the period lock enforced only inside postingEngine (posting-engine.service.ts:65 assertPostingAllowed).
- **Failure scenario:** Manual receivable of 100,000 TZS is created (posts DR AR 100,000 / CR Income). Operator calls PATCH /receivables/:id/record-payment {amount:100000}. Receivable outstanding -> 0, status PAID, customer.currentBalance drops by 100,000. But the GL AR-control account is still debited 100,000 with no offsetting credit and no cash recorded: the GL AR balance now exceeds the AR subledger by 100,000 forever, and cash on hand is understated. Trial-balance / AR-control-vs-subledger reconciliation breaks. Works even against a closed accounting period.
- **Suggested fix:** Mirror payables.recordPayment: accept a cashAccountId, and inside the transaction post a balanced settlement JE (DR Cash/Bank, CR AR_CONTROL) via postingEngine.postLines with referenceType 'Receivable'. Prefer routing all AR receipts through customer-payments.create (which already does this) and deprecate the JE-free recordPayment path.
- **Verifier:** Re-read all cited code; every load-bearing claim holds. (1) receivables.service.ts create() (lines 161-197) DOES post a real GL entry via postingEngine.postLines: DR AR_CONTROL / CR INCOME_SUMMARY for the full amount, and stores journalEntryId on the receivable. So the GL AR-control account is genuinely debited at creation — this refutes any "receivables never touch GL, so no imbalance" defense. (2) recordPayment() (lines 257-333) locks the row, computes nextOutstanding/nextPaid/nextStatus, calls tx.receivable.update, then syncSalesOrderPaymentFromReceivable + syncCustomerBalance, and returns. It never calls postingEngine.postLines — no CR to AR control, no DR to cash. (3) record-receivable-payment.dto.ts has only amount/paymentDate/notes — no cashAccountId — so it structurally cannot post a cash debit. (4) The sibling settlement paths do post: payables.service.ts:310-344 posts DR AP_CONTROL / CR CASH_ON_HAND ("ITMB-045"), and customer-payments.service.ts:267-292 posts DR Cash / CR AR / CR Advances. Receivables is the unfinished mirror. (5) The route is live and mutating: receivables.controller.ts:48-56 exposes PATCH :id/record-payment guarded by RequirePermissions('receivables.manage') — not dead code. (6) The period lock is enforced only inside the posting engine (posting-engine.service.ts postLines at 123-134 calls accountingControl.assertPostingAllowed; the event-based post() at 65 likewise). Because recordPayment skips postLines entirely, it also bypasses period-close enforcement. Failure scenario is exact: create a manual 100,000 TZS receivable (GL now DR AR 100,000), then PATCH record-payment {amount:100000} -> receivable outstanding 0/status PAID, customer.currentBalance drops 100,000, sales-order sync runs, but GL AR control stays debited 100,000 with no offsetting credit and no cash recorded. AR-control GL balance permanently exceeds the AR subledger by 100,000, cash is understated, trial balance / AR reconciliation breaks, and it works even in a closed period. Not a KNOWN INTENDED item (refunds fail-closed, overpayment model, automation gate, etc. are unrelated). Severity critical is appropriate: silent, permanent GL/subledger divergence on a core cash-collection path with a period-lock bypass.

### [HIGH] Audit adjustments post journal lines with no account validation (cross-company / inactive accounts and negative amounts accepted)

- **Domain:** Accounting engine & GL  |  **Type:** guard-gap  |  **File:** `backend/src/modules/audit-adjustments/audit-adjustments.service.ts:195`
- **Evidence:** post() writes JournalEntryLines directly via tx.journalEntryLine.createMany (L195-204) bypassing the PostingEngine. There is NO check that each line.accountId exists, is active, deletedAt=null, or belongs to existing.companyId — unlike PostingEngine.validatePostingLines (posting-engine.service.ts L218-230) and JournalEntriesService.validateLineAccounts (L101-120) which both enforce this. The DTO only validates accountId as @IsString (audit-adjustment.dto.ts L14-15). Additionally the balance check's toCents (L148-158) rejects only non-finite/>2dp values, NOT negatives, and the DTO uses @IsNumber with no @Min(0), so negative debits/credits are accepted (PostingEngine.toCents explicitly rejects numeric<0).
- **Failure scenario:** An approver posts an audit adjustment whose line accountId belongs to a DIFFERENT company (or an inactive/soft-deleted account). The JE and its lines are written with existing.companyId but reference a foreign/inactive account, corrupting the GL and leaking a cross-company account into the ledger. Alternatively, lines like {debit:-100} and {debit:100} sum-balance to zero and post negative debits the rest of the engine forbids.
- **Suggested fix:** Before posting, validate accounts exactly like JournalEntriesService.validateLineAccounts (count active, non-deleted accounts scoped to existing.companyId, require count===distinct ids), or route the posting through PostingEngine.postLines which enforces this. Reject negative debit/credit (add @Min(0) on the DTO and a numeric<0 guard in toCents).
- **Verifier:** Every claim in the finding is verified against the actual source and I could not refute it.

1) post() (audit-adjustments.service.ts L176-215) writes JournalEntryLines via tx.journalEntryLine.createMany (L195-204) using l.accountId directly, with NO chartOfAccount lookup. Grep of the whole service shows accountId only at L87, L199, L280 and NO chartOfAccount/isActive reference anywhere — so no existence/active/deletedAt/companyId check exists in create() or post().

2) The two cited siblings DO enforce this, confirming real divergence: PostingEngine.validatePostingLines (posting-engine.service.ts L218-230) counts chartOfAccount WHERE id in accountIds, companyId, isActive:true, deletedAt:null and throws if the count mismatches; JournalEntriesService.validateLineAccounts (journal-entries.service.ts L101-120) does the identical check.

3) Nothing compensates downstream. AccountingControlService.assertPostingAllowed (accounting-control.service.ts, full file) only validates the accounting period status/lock — it never touches chartOfAccount. The DB FK (schema.prisma L2057) is `account ChartOfAccount @relation(fields:[accountId], references:[id], onDelete:Restrict)` — it only requires the account row to EXIST; it does NOT constrain account.companyId == line.companyId, nor isActive, nor deletedAt. So a line whose accountId belongs to a DIFFERENT company (or an inactive/soft-deleted account) is accepted and written with existing.companyId, corrupting the GL and leaking a foreign account into the ledger.

4) Negative-amount claim confirmed. DTO (dto/audit-adjustment.dto.ts L20-28) declares debit/credit as @IsOptional @Type(()=>Number) @IsNumber() with NO @Min(0); accountId is @IsString only (L13-14). The service's toCents (L148-158) rejects only non-finite and >2dp values and does NOT reject negatives, unlike PostingEngine.toCents (L240: `numeric < 0` -> throw) and JournalEntries.toCents (L176: `numeric < 0` -> throw). audit-adjustments post() also lacks the per-line "only debit or credit, not both" and "non-zero" guards both siblings have. Its balance check (L159-167) only compares total debit cents vs total credit cents, so lines like {debit:-100} and {debit:100} sum-balance to zero and post negative debits the rest of the engine forbids.

This is a mutating, ledger-posting route with a real cross-company / inactive-account GL corruption path plus negative-amount acceptance; it is not on any KNOWN-INTENDED item. High severity is appropriate.
